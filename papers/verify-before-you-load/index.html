Verify Before You Load: Model Bills of Materials and Provenance Verification for Machine-Learning Models in CI/CD Pipelines

Pujitha Sri Lakshmi Paladugu
Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0001-5678-4022

Preprint · 2026-10-11 · 32 pages · Full text (PDF)

Abstract

Pre-trained machine-learning models are consumed like software dependencies and promoted through CI/CD pipelines, yet they arrive as bundles of weights, tokenizers, configuration and sometimes executable code, and documented incidents show that they are already being attacked. Standards for model bills of materials, model signing and build provenance now exist, but they are specified separately and enforcement is left to the consumer. This paper composes these standards into an executable, consumer-side admission gate and, unlike prior measurement studies and standards documents, evaluates the composition with fault injection that attributes each detection to an individual control. We derive an incident-grounded threat model and attack taxonomy, specify a verify-before-load pipeline as eight fail-closed policy rules placed at the pull, promotion, deployment and load stages, and implement it as an open-source Python prototype. Against 15 adversarial and 3 benign scenarios, the gate rejected 14 adversarial artifacts and admitted every benign one; hub-style checksum and pickle-scanning baselines rejected 2. A leave-one-out analysis shows that five scenarios are caught by exactly one control, so a simpler "is it signed?" policy misses real attacks. The one admitted artifact, a backdoored model from a compromised trusted builder, marks what signing cannot establish. Verification cost one SHA-256 pass (1.54 s for 2 GiB) plus about 2 ms of policy evaluation. A documentation review finds that no examined registry enforces signature verification on pull by default.
