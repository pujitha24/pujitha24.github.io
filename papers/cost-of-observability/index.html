The Cost of Observability: A Simulation Study of How Trace Sampling, Scrape Intervals, Cardinality Caps and Log Sampling Delay or Prevent Incident Detection

Pujitha Sri Lakshmi Paladugu
Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0001-5678-4022

Preprint · 2026-10-11 · 36 pages · Full text (PDF)

Abstract

Operators of cloud services reduce telemetry volume to control monitoring cost: they sample traces, lengthen metric scrape intervals, cap label cardinality and sample logs. Network management has long studied the accuracy–overhead trade-off of polling and packet sampling, but the effect of these service-level reductions on incident detection is rarely measured. We present a reproducible, seeded discrete-time simulation of a 30-service, 59-endpoint microservice system with heavy-tailed request rates and 2,000 Zipf-distributed tenants. We inject five incident classes: a global error spike, a low-traffic endpoint error, a 99th-percentile latency regression on one dependency, a gradual error creep and a single-tenant failure. Forty-three pipeline configurations and 1,247 combinations are evaluated under identical alert rules over 2,500 incident trials and 10 incident-free days, and volumes are priced with three public list-price books. Head sampling at 1% detected 4.8% of low-traffic endpoint failures, against 89.8% at full fidelity. Tail sampling matched full-fidelity detection in every class with 3.9% of the span volume and 25–35 s of added delay, provided its buffer had headroom above peak load. Severity-aware log sampling at 1% preserved error detection but not latency detection. Scrape intervals up to 120 s added at most 40 s of median delay. Capping the tenant label at 100 values reduced tenant-failure detection from 100% to 44%. Closed-form models of capture probability, scrape delay and cardinality dilution agree with the simulation. We map every pipeline to OpenTelemetry Collector and Prometheus configuration and give a procedure for choosing reduction settings.

Keywords: observability; distributed tracing; trace sampling; Prometheus; OpenTelemetry; incident detection; network management; monitoring cost
