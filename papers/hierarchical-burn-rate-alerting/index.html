Aggregation Hides Local Failure: Hierarchical, Evidence-Gated Burn-Rate Alerting for Global Edge Platforms

Pujitha Sri Lakshmi Paladugu
Independent Researcher · ORCID 0009-0001-5678-4022

Preprint · 2026-10-21 · 12 pages · Full text (PDF)

Abstract

Global edge platforms serve traffic from dozens to hundreds of points of presence (PoPs), and most operators alert on service-level objectives (SLOs) computed over fleet-wide aggregates. We show that this practice is structurally blind to localized failures. For a PoP carrying a fraction w of traffic and failing with error rate e, the fleet-wide burn rate is approximately w·e/β, where β is the error budget; a page fires only when this exceeds the burn threshold θ, so any PoP with w < θβ/e can fail indefinitely without paging. Under the widely used multi-window, multi-burn-rate rules (θ = 14.4 for a 99.9% objective), a PoP returning errors on half of its requests must carry more than 2.9% of global traffic to be detected, and in a heavy-tailed fleet of 60 PoPs about 85% of PoPs fall below that bound. We propose hierarchical, evidence-gated burn-rate alerting, which keeps the fleet-wide rules and adds per-PoP rules guarded by a persistence condition, a Wilson lower-confidence bound on the PoP error ratio, and a minimum-failure count. In a reproducible simulation study (60 PoPs; 240 injected incidents; 168 simulated incident-free days), fleet-wide alerting detected 17% of single-PoP failures and none affecting the smallest quartile of PoPs, while the proposed scheme detected all of them with a median delay of 10 minutes and produced no false pages; naive per-PoP alerting detected them in 1 minute but generated 0.54 false pages per week. We give the analysis, the design, its mapping to common monitoring systems, and the simulation code.

Keywords: site reliability engineering; service-level objectives; error budgets; burn-rate alerting; edge computing; content delivery networks; observability; gray failure
